Vacuum Pump Wall Climbing Suit

A wearable, battery-powered vacuum system for climbing flat vertical walls, with four suction pads, diagonal release controls, and real-time pressure monitoring that tells the climber when it's safe to move.

View full project on GitHub

Overview

The first version used two all-in-one vacuum pads, one per hand, with foot loops. Losing suction on either pad meant coming off the wall, and climbing meant moving a hand and foot on the same side together, which was exhausting.

The second version moves the electronics into a backpack frame and gives each limb its own pad and pump. Releasing an arm pad with the button in its handgrip also releases the diagonally opposite leg pad, so the climber moves in a natural cross-crawl while the other diagonal pair holds the wall.

Pressure sensors on every line feed an Arduino that lights a green LED on the forearm only when both pads of a diagonal pair are holding past the safe threshold, so the climber never has to guess whether it's safe to let go.

Project facts

Type
Personal hardware project, about 2.5 years of development
Power
2× 5000 mAh 12 V LiPo batteries
Vacuum
4× 12 V pumps rated to −85 kPa, one per pad
Safety threshold
−9 psi on both pads of a diagonal pair
Control
Arduino with pressure sensors, solenoid valves and LED indicators

Design evolution

Version 1 relied on two pads and manual foot loops, creating a single point of failure.

Electronics and safety system

The −9 psi threshold comes from a 170 lb climber, a 2× safety factor and a conservative 0.5 friction coefficient. Spread over one arm pad and one leg pad (96 in²), that needs about 7.1 psi, which was rounded up to allow for leaks and imperfect seals.

Pad construction

Each pad is a partially hollowed wooden plank sealed with a rubber door gasket and a ring of high-density foam, encapsulated in silicone for an airtight, high-friction seal. Arm pads mount to wrist guards with metal handgrips, and leg pads use hockey-pad harnesses with foot straps that carry most of the climber's weight.
