Farnsworth Fusor

A working demonstration fusion reactor, with a custom high-voltage transformer and rectifier circuit and a vacuum chamber that sustains a stable plasma.

Overview

As a personal engineering project, I built a working demonstration fusion reactor, a Farnsworth fusor, using a custom-built high-voltage transformer and rectifier circuit to generate and sustain a stable plasma field.

Project facts

Type
Personal engineering project
Power
Custom high-voltage transformer and rectifier circuit
Vacuum
Custom chamber holding −15 psi
Method
Inertial Electrostatic Confinement (IEC)
Result
Stable plasma with presumed low-level fusion

The build

I engineered and built a vacuum chamber system that could hold −15 psi, creating the low-pressure environment needed for Inertial Electrostatic Confinement (IEC), where a high-voltage grid at the centre of the chamber accelerates ions inward toward each other.

I also isolated and shielded the high-voltage components to minimize electrical interference, prevent short circuits, and keep the system reliable and safe to operate.

Results

With this project, I achieved and maintained a controlled, low-level presumed fusion reaction. That said, this fusor likely produces extremely little fusion and is not a practical energy source. It also poses no serious radiation hazard at the voltages and pressures used, especially without a fusion fuel gas such as deuterium or tritium, which would vastly boost the reaction.
